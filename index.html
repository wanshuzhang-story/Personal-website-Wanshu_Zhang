01 | Completed pilot | Open evaluation materials

EpiTrace

A controlled evaluation of how language models assess repeated reports, independent support and unresolved source relationships. I designed matched fictional document packets and evaluated Apertus 8B and 70B.

In each model, all 39 packets with unspecified source relationships were labelled dependent rather than unknown under the benchmark rubric. Those packets shared one template; broader testing and independent review are needed to assess generalisation.

PRESTO

A two-phase workflow for discovering and tracking rumoured events in historical newspapers. Its extraction study recovers propositions from passages explicitly marked by rumour or rumor vocabulary, asking what newspapers themselves called rumour.

An extended journal article comparing rule-based and language-model extraction is in preparation.

03 | Open research toolkit | Prototype

RumourFlow

A review-oriented toolkit for inspecting source attribution and textual reuse in historical newspapers. It combines source cues, newspaper-title gazetteers, text similarity and clustering to produce reviewable candidates and network-ready exports.

Candidate links remain open to source checking: shared wording alone does not establish who copied whom.

Research questions

Historical concepts. Computational tests.

My PhD develops computationally usable ways to identify and analyse rumour, while asking how reporting history enters later assessments of evidence.

Q1

What did newspapers call rumour?

I study the information explicitly given this label and its relationship to hoax, misinformation, satire, parody and propaganda. Semantic comparison, extraction and annotation help make these distinctions testable without treating a label, a credibility judgment and eventual truth as interchangeable.

Q2

When do earlier reports matter?

My ongoing Livingstone study examines how newspapers invoke earlier false or disputed death reports when assessing later reports, and whether those references accompany demands for additional corroboration. This doctoral work develops the framework of rumour-induced epistemic contamination.

Q3

What should AI preserve about evidence?

EpiTrace tests source dependence and unresolved origins. Building on this work, I am developing a broader research direction on how AI-assisted assessments can preserve qualifications and revise conclusions as the available evidence changes.

In development

Public annotation of historical rumour sources

I am building a platform through which public contributors can annotate historical sources and question the categories used to interpret them. The aim is to keep observations linked to their sources and disagreements visible, rather than treating agreement as proof of a claim.
